# Assurance

> **Status:** built in v0.4.0-dev.1. The run-graph, gate and assess kernels are proved over the shipped Rust, and the run graph is model-checked with TLC. On 16 November 2026 the proofs were extended to the decisions that reach "met", "ready" and "allowed" through other paths: the digest comparisons that make a receipt or a statement about the present, a step's advance on its gates as found, the checklist roll-up, deliveries and the delivery guard, guard rules and their requirements' facts, and what grounds evidence. The other kernels added since v0.5.0-dev.1 (investigation, decision states, checker verdicts, the command reader, `find`, `idle`, `diag`, `walk`, scope globs) are tested, not proved; the table below says how. The command reader's tables and the guard conformance cases added in v0.6.0-dev.1. The policy round (16 November 2026, built in v0.6.0-dev.3) proved a team's `accept`, pins and drift, how a step closed and how a plan ended, `plan skip`'s lock and the close check, and evidence bound to its run and subject.

What stands behind `writfold kb`, sorted by how strong the claim is. The short version: the write protocol and the run graph are model-checked, the write protocol is kill-tested; the decisions about plans, gates, checklists, deliveries, guard rules and evidence (which step is current, whether a step may be recorded as done, whether a receipt or a statement is about the present bytes, whether a checklist is covered, whether a candidate is ready, whether a guarded call passes, what grounds a citation) are proved for every input over the shipped Rust; everything that turns files into those decisions' inputs is tested.

## Model-checked

[`spec/KbWrite.tla`](../../spec/KbWrite.tla) models the write protocol and the index refresh: two appenders and a creator, two refreshers, a reader, and a power loss at any moment. TLC checks five invariants over about 18 million states: a published record is complete, no acknowledged write is lost, create-new never overwrites, the stored index is the derivation of its pin, and a reader told `index=fresh` sees exactly that derivation. Five mutants, each dropping one protocol step, each fail their invariant.

[`spec/RunGraph.tla`](../../spec/RunGraph.tla) models the run graph (`plan set|use|advance|skip`): two sessions, either in two clones that merge or in one clone, a crash between any two writes, the subject's bytes moving under a gate, and a template edited mid-run. TLC checks that a step has at most one `done-` record, that every done record names a receipt about the bytes it judged, that every splice has its snapshot, that two clones holding the same files agree on the plan, that the current step is the first neither done nor skipped, that a splice repeats no template already spliced on its chain unless `--again` asked, that the done set only grows, that a step adds nodes only before the current step, and that a stale step never becomes current again. The bounds are small (two templates, splices two levels deep, two or three decisions per session): with drift and a crash the check is complete for two sessions in one clone (about 44 million states), and for two merging clones it is complete without them (about 2 million states) and was stopped unfinished with them. Ten mutants, each changing one rule, each fail their property. Details and numbers: [design §27](../writfold/kb-v1-design.md#27-model-specrungraphtla).

The run-graph model found two gaps; both were fixed in v0.4.0-dev.1 ([design §28](../writfold/kb-v1-design.md#28-plan-write-re-check-and-merge-disorder-flags-v040-dev1)):

- **Within one clone,** a plan write used to decide from a fold read outside the write lock. Each plan write now re-checks its decision under the lock (the `Op::Plan` guard), and refuses, writing nothing, when another session changed the plan meanwhile. `RunGraph.same` checks that re-checked protocol.
- **Across clones,** a merge can still put open steps before a done one, or set two plans on one run; nothing is lost and both clones agree. `plan status` now flags it: `!out-of-order`, `!two-plans` and `!repeated`.

Both are tested (`crates/writfold-kb/tests/plan_recheck.rs`, `plan_merge.rs`); the model was not re-run against the Rust.

The models are hand-written and bounded. They are evidence about the design, not a refinement of the Rust: `KbWrite.tla` is tied to the code by the kill tests at the same steps, and `RunGraph.tla` by the plan tests (order independence, kill tests, the kernel's exhaustive tests), not by proof.

## Proved

With Charon and Aeneas, over the shipped Rust, for every input ([`aeneas.md`](../writfold/aeneas.md), `proofs/Writfold/`):

| Kernel | Proved |
|---|---|
| The run graph (`writfold_core::plan`) | the current step is the first step neither done nor skipped; `plan advance` decides on the step's gates as found (`plan::advance`): it is admitted exactly when the step is the current one and every gate it names has a usable definition and stands met, and is otherwise refused in a fixed order (complete, not current, the first owed gate); a gate with no usable definition is never met; a step with no gates owes nothing; `stale` is exactly a done step with a gate not met |
| The splice model (`writfold_core::plan::splice`, `splice_check`) | a splice keeps every existing step in order with its state and the done prefix, inserts open steps, and makes the first of them current; a splice is `already` exactly when its key is already spliced at the current step or above it, so repeating it changes nothing; an admitted splice is never deeper than 4. Production places spliced steps in the fold, which is tested against this model (above), not proved |
| About the present (`writfold_core::gate`: `about`, `evidence`, `judge`, `statements`) | a receipt, a statement or a cover row is about the present exactly when its recorded definition and content digests are the present ones, byte for byte; a receipt also needs readable (non-empty) present content. A gate's state is `satisfied` exactly when its newest receipt about the present definition and content passed, so receipts about other bytes never satisfy whatever they record; an attestation or a waiver stands only when it is about the present |
| Gate obligations (`writfold_core::gate`) | only exit status 0 on a subject that did not move is a pass, any other exit status a failure; an attestation or a waiver never yields `satisfied`, only standing as itself; met is exactly satisfied, attested or waived |
| Checklist gates (`writfold_core::checklist`) | an item is covered exactly when its newest row is about the present and is supported by at least one grounded reference, or not applicable where the checklist's policy takes that, or none found (citing at least one look that still found nothing) where it takes that; an item whose policy rejects not-applicable is never covered by it; a checklist is covered exactly when every required item is, and optional items change nothing; it stands `attested` exactly when covered, is never `satisfied`, and is met exactly when covered or a current waiver stands |
| A team's policy (`writfold_core::policy`, built in v0.6.0-dev.3) | a standing meets a gate exactly when it is a met word the gate's `accept` takes; without `accept` this is the rule above; a policy only narrows; a gate that rejects waivers is never met by one, whatever statements exist; a pinned digest is the same only when the same bytes are there now, one moved pin moves the run, and an empty pin is never "same"; a step reads `passed` only when done with every gate passed; the plan's word follows the table in [run-graph](run-graph.md#policy-unskippable-steps-pins-and-how-a-plan-ended) row by row, so a moved policy, an open step, a skip, a waiver, an attestation or a repin never reads as plain `complete` |
| Skipping and closing (`writfold_core::plan`, built in v0.6.0-dev.3) | `plan skip` is admitted exactly for the current step the run's pin does not lock, refused in the order complete, not current, locked; a skip record of a locked step never closes it, so it stays current; the plan's last open step closes only when every required template is present |
| Evidence bound to its run (`writfold_core::gate::state`, built in v0.6.0-dev.3) | a receipt from another run, about another repository or candidate, or with a broken digest counts as absent: with none bound the gate is `open`, and the state is exactly the state of the bound receipts alone |
| Deliveries (`writfold_core::delivery`) | the deciding candidate is the newest one no done record names; a delivery is met exactly when its mode declares at least one obligation and every one is met, and `satisfied` only when every one is a passing check; a candidate is ready on the same rule, so a mode that declares nothing is never ready; a passed check whose cited evidence moved is stale; the delivery guard passes known bytes only when a ready candidate holds them, and unknown bytes only when there is a candidate and every one is ready |
| Guard rules (`writfold_core::guard`) | a rule passes exactly when it did not match or every requirement is met, so a requirement that could not be judged never lets a matched rule pass; several rules combine to the strictest decision (deny, then ask, then pass); an audit rule never decides the call; a call is denied exactly when an enforcing deny rule has an unmet requirement; each requirement's fact from what was found (`run.current` only for an open run; `step.met` for a run with no plan, no current step or a current step that owes nothing, and unknown when the plan cannot be read; `gate:<id>` only for a defined gate that is met; `gates.met` only when something is owed, all of it is met and nothing is invalid); the Stop nudge never fires while a stop hook is continuing |
| What grounds evidence (`writfold_core::ground`, `writfold_core::observe`) | a waiver is never evidence; a gate reference grounds only while the gate is satisfied by a check (attested while it stands attested); a gate receipt only while it is about this subject, the current definition and the present content, passed, and the gate stands met; a retrieval only with at least one citation, all fresh; a citation token only as exactly one fresh citation; an observation only for an `ok` look with at least one fresh citation |
| The assessed gate (`writfold_core::assess`) | a check meets the gate exactly when Weft entailed it and every assumption it used is grounded; a hypothetical entailment is never a pass; an incomplete run or an empty requirement list is unknown |

Put together, and proved as one theorem (`done_only_when_met`): a step is recorded as done only when it is the current step and each of its gates has a usable definition and stands either on a passing newest receipt whose recorded definition and content digests equal the present ones, or on an attestation or a waiver about those digests. That the kernels are given the right digests (which receipts are valid and in this run, how they are ordered, which gates a step names, the digests themselves) is tested.

The proofs are calibrated: every decision function behind these claims has at least one mutant of its Rust source (a dropped check, a flipped condition, a loop that skips the last element) that the proofs must reject, failing exactly the theorem the mutant names; the headline statements are corollaries of those calibrated specifications. The mutants are listed with their theorems in [`aeneas.md`](../writfold/aeneas.md#the-claims-and-their-mutants). Every theorem's axioms are audited (Lean's three standard axioms only), and every theorem a mutant names is a required claim of the audit, so a rename cannot pass silently.

**Not proved:** the fold from event and pin files to the list of steps (and with it where a splice is placed, which steps a pin locks, how each step closed), reading and writing pin files, which observation statuses count as a look that found nothing, template and gate-definition parsing (`accept`, `not_applicable`, `none_found`, `unskippable` included), digests, Git and file reads, the write protocol's code, scope globs (which paths a gate's scope covers), citation verdicts (`writfold_core::verdict`), decision states and investigations, the bounded walk (`writfold_core::walk`), the command reader, and the ordering of receipts. They are tested (above).

## Trusted, not checked

1. **The agent cooperates.** It can skip a gate, edit a tracked file or write a receipt by hand. Git history shows it; nothing prevents it.
2. **`--by` is text.** A statement records who was named, not who typed it. The Git commit that adds the file is the record of who committed it.
3. **Hooks are best effort.** They may not fire, they time out open, and the harness can change them ([hooks](hooks.md)).
4. **The model's premises:** rename and link are atomic, fsync makes content durable, SQLite transactions are atomic and durable, a digest identifies content.
5. **Not modelled:** another tool editing a KB file without the write lock (the re-check narrows that window and does not close it), and a file reverted to earlier bytes between two observations.
6. **For the proofs:** Charon and Aeneas at their pins translate the Rust faithfully; the Aeneas library's models of the Rust operations used are right; the Lean kernel and its three standard axioms. The proofs are about the kernel functions; the code that calls them is tested, not proved. `splice` is proved for plans whose new length fits in `usize`.
7. **Git and the file system** behave as documented.

## Not determined at all

Model output, the quality of a statement or a reflection, whether a recorded decision is right, and whether a delivery draft is true. The delivery checks count words, match patterns and re-check citations; they do not read for meaning.

## What "derived" buys

Gate standing, delivery standing and the index are recomputed from tracked files on every read and never stored. Two clones at the same commit with the same local repositories give the same answer, and there is no state to migrate or repair. Where several gate receipts cover the same bytes, the newest is chosen by a `seq` counter and the file name, never the clock ([design 21.7](../writfold/kb-v1-design.md#217-receipt-order-without-a-clock)). One caveat today: delivery receipts, assessed receipts and statements are still ordered by their recorded time, which comes from the machine's clock.
