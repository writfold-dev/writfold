The backfill's second round (17 November 2026, built in v0.6.0-dev.4) proved which paths a gate's scope covers, the plan writes' re-check under the lock and the closed run, decision states and investigations, citation verdicts and check receipts, the walk's `coverage=complete` claim, the lint's verdict, `find`'s prefix tier and its rows, which command a guard rule names, idle runs, and the order receipts are judged in; assessed and delivery receipts now carry `seq` and are ordered without a clock. The delivery round (17 November 2026, built in v0.6.0-dev.4) proved each delivery obligation met under its own policy, which candidate decides once candidates are withdrawn or bound, the `posted:<mode>` standing, and the delivery guard's decision over every candidate of the run.

What stands behind `writfold kb`, sorted by how strong the claim is. The short version: the write protocol and the run graph are model-checked, the write protocol is kill-tested; the decisions about plans, gates, checklists, deliveries, guard rules, evidence, scopes and citations (which step is current, whether a step may be recorded as done, whether a receipt or a statement is about the present bytes, which paths a gate's scope covers, whether a checklist is covered, whether a candidate is ready, whether a guarded call passes, what grounds a citation, whether a citation is fresh, which receipt decides) are proved for every input over the shipped Rust; everything that turns files into those decisions' inputs is tested.

## Model-checked

[`spec/KbWrite.tla`](../../spec/KbWrite.tla) models the write protocol and the index refresh: two appenders and a creator, two refreshers, a reader, and a power loss at any moment. TLC checks five invariants over about 18 million states: a published record is complete, no acknowledged write is lost, create-new never overwrites, the stored index is the derivation of its pin, and a reader told `index=fresh` sees exactly that derivation. Five mutants, each dropping one protocol step, each fail their invariant.

[`spec/Loop.tla`](../../spec/Loop.tla) (from v0.6.0-dev.15) models `plan again`: sessions in one clone or in two that merge count a loop's rounds outside the lock and publish create-new under it, with a crash. TLC checks that rounds never exceed `max` in a clone and are numbered without gaps; the mutant without the kernel's bound fails. A probe shows the bound also holds without the re-check: a round's file name is its ordinal, so a second session racing for the last round finds the name taken, and two clones that each add the same round meet as a merge conflict, not as `max + 1` rounds.

[`spec/Invocation.tla`](../../spec/Invocation.tla) (from v0.6.0-dev.22, #145) models an agent invocation and the report bound to it: `agent prepare` records an invocation at the current step with its closure digest, and `deliver prepare` records the step current where the report is delivered (from v0.6.0-dev.24, #164); two invocations of one step, so a retry is in the model; a worker that may deliver any payload for any step at any time; the `invocation.bound` check; `plan advance` deciding outside the lock and re-checking under it; an edit to the definitions; withdrawal; a crash; and two clones that merge. TLC checks that the check passes only with the invocation recorded in that clone, that two live payloads for one invocation make neither ready, that every done record was written while its report was ready, that an invocation never binds a report to another step, that delivering or checking never advances, that no done record is ever removed, and (`CheckIsStable`) that a report passing the check still passes after `plan advance`. In v0.6.0-dev.24 the check is complete over about 58 million distinct states. Five mutants (no single-report rule, no re-check under the lock, no step comparison, delivery that advances, and the step judged as the run's current one instead of the recorded one) each fail their property. A probe ([trace](../../spec/Invocation.probe-MergeAfterDone.trace.txt)) shows the honest outcome of a merge: a step done on one report in one clone, a different report for the same invocation from the other, and after the merge the step is stale and still done. Not modelled, because Writfold does not do them: launching, cancelling or acknowledging a worker, budgets and delegation; no progress property is claimed. Also not modelled: a crash between the invocation record and its package (the record's own write is the write protocol's, covered by its kill tests).

[`spec/KbRepair.tla`](../../spec/KbRepair.tla) (from v0.6.0-dev.14) models the other way an index is published: a whole new file built beside it and renamed over it, as `index rebuild` and the repair of `index verify` do. A writer, a store that can corrupt the index, the transactional refresher and a verifier that builds a shadow, compares and repairs, with a crash at any moment. TLC checks that a healthy index is always the derivation of its pin, that a repair installs only a derivation, and that a repair replaces only an index that was broken (never one that was merely behind or that a refresh had just replaced). Two mutants, dropping the source re-check or the live-generation check, each fail their invariant. The model's first draft found a real bug: a repair that re-checked only the pin could replace a healthy index committed during the comparison ([#58](https://github.com/writfold-dev/writfold/issues/58)).

[`spec/RunGraph.tla`](../../spec/RunGraph.tla) models the run graph (`plan set|use|advance|skip`): two sessions, either in two clones that merge or in one clone, a crash between any two writes, the subject's bytes moving under a gate, and a template edited mid-run. TLC checks that a step has at most one `done-` record, that every done record names a receipt about the bytes it judged, that every splice has its snapshot, that two clones holding the same files agree on the plan, that the current step is the first neither done nor skipped, that a splice repeats no template already spliced on its chain unless `--again` asked, that the done set only grows, that a step adds nodes only before the current step, and that a stale step never becomes current again. The bounds are small (two templates, splices two levels deep, two or three decisions per session): with drift and a crash the check is complete for two sessions in one clone (about 44 million states), and for two merging clones it is complete without them (about 2 million states) and was stopped unfinished with them. Ten mutants, each changing one rule, each fail their property. Details and numbers: [design §27](../writfold/kb-v1-design.md#27-model-specrungraphtla).

The run-graph model found two gaps; both were fixed in v0.4.0-dev.1 ([design §28](../writfold/kb-v1-design.md#28-plan-write-re-check-and-merge-disorder-flags-v040-dev1)):

- **Within one clone,** a plan write used to decide from a fold read outside the write lock. Each plan write now re-checks its decision under the lock (the `Op::Plan` guard), and refuses, writing nothing, when another session changed the plan meanwhile. `RunGraph.same` checks that re-checked protocol.
- **Across clones,** a merge can still put open steps before a done one, or set two plans on one run; nothing is lost and both clones agree. `plan status` now flags it: `!out-of-order`, `!two-plans` and `!repeated`.

Both are tested (`crates/writfold-kb/tests/plan_recheck.rs`, `plan_merge.rs`); the model was not re-run against the Rust.

TLC runs in no gate: the model checks are run by hand when a model changes, with the pinned `tla2tools.jar` of the tools image (`java -cp tla2tools.jar tlc2.TLC -config spec/<Model>.cfg spec/<Model>.tla`, and each `*.mutant-*.cfg` and `*.probe-*.cfg`, which must report the violation its comment names). The numbers on this page are from those runs.

The models are hand-written and bounded. They are evidence about the design, not a refinement of the Rust: `KbWrite.tla` is tied to the code by the kill tests at the same steps, and `RunGraph.tla` by the plan tests (order independence, kill tests, the kernel's exhaustive tests), not by proof.

Put together, and proved as one theorem (`done_only_when_met`): a step is recorded as done only when it is the current step and each of its gates has a usable definition and stands either on a passing newest receipt whose recorded definition and content digests equal the present ones, or on an attestation or a waiver about those digests. That the kernels are given the right digests (which receipts are valid and in this run, which gates a step names, the digests themselves) is tested; the receipts are sorted with the proved `(seq, name)` order.

The proofs are calibrated: every decision function behind these claims has at least one mutant of its Rust source (a dropped check, a flipped condition, a loop that skips the last element) that the proofs must reject, failing exactly the theorem the mutant names; the headline statements are corollaries of those calibrated specifications. The mutants are listed with their theorems in [`aeneas.md`](../writfold/aeneas.md#the-claims-and-their-mutants). Every theorem's axioms are audited (Lean's three standard axioms only), and every theorem a mutant names is a required claim of the audit, so a rename cannot pass silently.

**Not proved:** the fold from event and pin files to the list of steps (and with it where a splice is placed, which steps a pin locks, how each step closed), reading and writing pin files, which observation statuses count as a look that found nothing, template and gate-definition parsing (`accept`, `not_applicable`, `none_found`, `unskippable` included), digests, Git and file reads, the write protocol's code, decoding text into the scope and `find` kernels' inputs, gathering a citation's facts, the reads that establish a decision state change's facts (the commit on its ref, the evidence grounding, the successor present, readiness; which flags a change takes and whether those facts are enough are proved), the rows each `find` tier gathers and SQLite's ranking within a tier (the tier order and the rows shown are proved), the SQL that finds the files a key change moved and the index's other SQL, the bounded walk's traversal, the command reader, the order of statements, and for deliveries: reading `waivable`, `audience`, `rendering` and `routes`, finding a body flag or a tool field and where a command acts, the rendering itself, which withdrawals and post records belong to the candidate and run, and drawing findings. They are tested (above).

The laws those decisions obey are stated once, in plain Lean independent of the Rust (`proofs/Calculus`): unknown never admits, adding an obligation never makes an unmet set met, a word that claims all of something holds only when nothing was left unaccounted, and knowing more only ever refines an `unverifiable` answer. A kernel is linked when a theorem shows its translation implements the operation a law is about; [the laws page](../writfold/laws.md) gives every law with its statement. Since v0.6.0-dev.29 every translated function is linked or declared out with a reason, and both gates refuse one that is neither (#213).

## Trusted, not checked

1. **The agent cooperates.** It can skip a gate, edit a tracked file or write a receipt by hand. Git history shows it; nothing prevents it.
2. **`--by` is text.** A statement records who was named, not who typed it. The Git commit that adds the file is the record of who committed it.
3. **Hooks are best effort.** They may not fire, they time out open, and the harness can change them ([hooks](hooks.md)).
4. **The model's premises:** rename and link are atomic, fsync makes content durable, SQLite transactions are atomic and durable, a digest identifies content.
5. **Not modelled:** another tool editing a KB file without the write lock (the re-check narrows that window and does not close it), and a file reverted to earlier bytes between two observations.
6. **For the proofs:** Charon and Aeneas at their pins translate the Rust faithfully; the Aeneas library's models of the Rust operations used are right; the Lean kernel and its three standard axioms. The proofs are about the kernel functions; the code that calls them is tested, not proved. `splice` is proved for plans whose new length fits in `usize`.
7. **Git and the file system** behave as documented.
8. **A post record is text.** `deliver posted` records that someone said the payload was posted, where (`--ref`, never fetched) and by whom (`--by`). Writfold sent nothing and saw no post: a record can name a post that did not happen, and a post made without a record leaves no trace. The guard and `posted:<mode>` rely on the record as given.

## Not determined at all

Model output, the quality of a statement or a reflection, whether a recorded decision is right, and whether a delivery draft is true. The delivery checks count words, match patterns and re-check citations; they do not read for meaning.

## Limits

What Writfold cannot enforce, and how it says so. These are limits of principle, not gaps a later release closes ([the foundation RFC](../rfcs/2026-11-27-referents-remainders-sites.md#what-writfold-cannot-enforce-and-how-it-says-so)).

| Cannot | Why | What is offered instead |
|---|---|---|
| Know that a model read or understood a file, a skill or an instruction | reading is not an event | a harness-reported read of these bytes (reported); "this package was written" (first hand); a review of the result (attested) |
| Judge anything a harness never reports: a call with no hook, a skill injected without a tool call, a harness with no binding | Writfold is not on the path of a call it is not shown | a [binding](hooks.md#any-harness-guard-check) declares the kinds its harness reports (`reports`); `guard check` for a harness or wrapper that can call it |
| Know what an arbitrary program does: `bash -c`, `eval`, a script, `ssh` | deciding it is running it | a guard reads the command line; such a call passes unless a rule names it |
| Hold back text shown to the user in chat | it is displayed before any hook can refuse it | a deliverable goes through `deliver prepare`; a Stop check of the last message is a recipe, after the fact |
| Keep a session from ending | a harness caps continuations (eight in Claude Code) and a crash ends it anyway | a [blocking stop rule](hooks.md#a-stop-rule-that-blocks) with a stated limit; the inbox at the next start; the [receiver](receiver.md) |
| Fail closed when the hook process never starts, or is killed at the harness's timeout | the harness lets that call through | the plugin's posture option exits 2 when the binary is missing; a [deadline](hooks.md#failing-open) inside the binary below the harness's timeout |
| Know who made a statement | no server, no signing: `--by`, a session id and an actor are recorded as given | the Git author; a bound report from a prepared invocation; at a receiver, re-execution |
| Stop a worker that edits its own rules, ledger or records | anyone who can write the clone can write them | detection in the recorded tier (`policy moved`), refusal at the [receiver](receiver.md) |

Every guard output says "a guard, not enforcement". A posture of `deny` does not change that: it narrows what a cooperating session can do by mistake.

## What "derived" buys

Gate standing, delivery standing and the index are recomputed from tracked files on every read and never stored. Two clones at the same commit with the same local repositories give the same answer, and there is no state to migrate or repair. Where several receipts cover the same bytes (gate receipts, cover rows, statements since v0.6.0-dev.12, and since v0.6.0-dev.4 delivery check receipts), the newest is chosen by a `seq` counter and the file name, never the clock ([design 21.7](../writfold/kb-v1-design.md#217-receipt-order-without-a-clock)); a receipt written before it carried `seq` reads as 0 and comes first, in file-name order. Since v0.6.0-dev.12 statements (attestations and waivers, a gate's or a delivery's) are ordered the same way, and the one that stands is the newest under that order among those about the present (proved: `newest_is_max`); a statement written before it carried `seq` reads as 0 and comes first, in file-name order.
